## What counts as a genuine finding

- **Over-optimistic claim:** the projection asserts "no disruption," "minimal impact," "easily managed," or similar for a category where a real entity above (a person, an obligation, a constraint) plainly conflicts with that claim, but the projection never names the conflict.
- **Omitted second-order effect:** a real entity above is affected in a way the projection's own claims do not mention at all — not a restatement of what the projection already said, a genuinely new consequence.
- Do not invent a finding that requires information not present above. If a real entity above genuinely does conflict with a projection claim, name that entity specifically — never a generic "this could be disruptive."
- If, after real adversarial scrutiny against the data above, the projection genuinely holds up, say so plainly in `revised_confidence_note` — do not manufacture a finding just to have one.

## Output

Respond with ONLY a single JSON object, no markdown fences, no
commentary, matching exactly this shape:
